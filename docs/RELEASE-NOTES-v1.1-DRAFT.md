## THE NOTES (this is the part a player reads)

### Five ways to watch him

Press **C**. The camera you already had stays first in the ring, then a chase camera that follows
him from behind, a close one over his shoulder, a high one that looks down across the dunes, and
first person, from inside his head. Press C again and you come back around to where you started.

### A glide you deploy in the air

The jump and the glide used to share one press, so starting a glide cost you a jump. Now the jump is
just a jump: tap **SPACE** for a short one, hold it for a higher one. Press SPACE a **second time
while he is off the ground** and he glides when he lands. It takes one session to learn, and then you
keep doing it on purpose.

The old way is still one click away in the panel, under Character, if you prefer it.

### The desert has a map, and places on it

Press **E**. Two places are named on it: the plains you start in, and the mountains, roughly 600
metres out. Press 1 or 2, or click a name, and you are standing there. When you land, the desert
tells you where you are.

### Slides are counted now

Ride down a slipface and the game keeps a record: how many slides you have taken, and the longest
one you have held.

### He does not disappear behind a dune

Pile up a wall or drop behind a ridge and he shows through it as a faint tracing instead of
vanishing. You can always tell which side of the sand he is standing on.

### The dash is steadier

At speed the figure used to wobble slightly. The camera was composing his picture one step behind
where he actually was, every frame. It is not any more.

### A menu for the sand, in the corner

Digging and building, six brushes, brush size and wet sand, all in the bottom-left corner while you
work, instead of a panel you have to open and close.

### What has not changed

The sand. The same angle of repose, the same avalanches that run and stop, the same music and wind
generated by the page with nothing downloaded.
